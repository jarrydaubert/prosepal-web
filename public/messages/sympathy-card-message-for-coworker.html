Sympathy Card Messages for a Coworker

Updated January 2026 • 4 min read

Writing a sympathy card for a coworker requires a delicate balance. You want to be genuine and supportive, but also respectful of professional boundaries. The relationship you have at work is different from personal friendships, yet moments of loss transcend those boundaries. Here's how to find the right words.

Tips for Writing Sympathy Messages for Coworker

What to Keep in Mind

  • Keep It Professional but Warm: You don't need to share deeply personal sentiments. Focus on acknowledging their loss and offering practical support, like covering their workload while they're away.
  • Don't Assume You Know Their Grief: Avoid phrases like "I know how you feel" even if you've experienced loss. Everyone's grief is unique. Instead, simply acknowledge that this is a difficult time.
  • Offer Specific Help: Rather than "Let me know if you need anything," offer something concrete: "I'll handle the Henderson account while you're out" or "I'll make sure you don't come back to a full inbox."

Short Sympathy Messages

When you want something brief but meaningful:

"Thinking of you during this difficult time. Please don't worry about anything here—we've got you covered."
"I'm so sorry for your loss. Take all the time you need."
"My deepest condolences. Wishing you strength and peace."

Medium-Length Messages

For when you want to say a bit more:

"I was so sorry to hear about your loss. Please know that we're all thinking of you and your family. Take whatever time you need—everything here will be handled."
"My heart goes out to you during this difficult time. I can only imagine how hard this must be. Please don't hesitate to reach out if there's anything I can do."
"I want you to know how sorry I am for your loss. When you're ready to come back, we'll all be here. Until then, focus on yourself and your family."

Heartfelt Messages

When you want to express deeper feelings:

"I don't have the right words—no one does for something like this. I just want you to know that I'm here, the team is here, and we're all holding space for you. Take care of yourself."
"Losing someone you love changes everything. I'm so deeply sorry you're going through this. Please know that you don't have to face any of this alone—not here, not anywhere. We're all thinking of you."
"I wish I could do more than send words, but please know they come with genuine care. Your [loved one] was lucky to have you. Take all the time you need—we'll be here when you're ready."
"There's nothing I can say to make this easier, and I won't try to. Just know that my thoughts are with you, and the whole team is ready to support you however we can."

When to Send Your Sympathy Card

Send your sympathy card as soon as you learn of the loss—ideally within a few days. If your coworker is out of the office, you can mail it to their home address (if known and appropriate) or leave it with HR to forward. Consider signing a group card from the team as well as sending a personal note if you're close.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I mention the deceased by name?

Yes, if you knew their name. Using the name of the person who passed shows you care and makes the message more personal. If you didn't know the person, "your loved one" or "your [mother/father/etc.]" works well.

Is it appropriate to send flowers to a coworker?

Flowers are generally appropriate, especially from a team or department. Individual flowers are also fine if you're close. Check with HR or the coworker's close friends about any preferences or cultural considerations.

What if I didn't know them well?

That's okay—your card can be brief. Even a short note saying "I'm sorry for your loss. Thinking of you." shows you care. You don't need a deep relationship to offer genuine condolences.
