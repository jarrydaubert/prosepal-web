Wedding Card Messages for a Friend

Updated February 2026 • 4 min read

Your friend found their person—and they invited you to celebrate. Now you're staring at a blank wedding card, trying to write something better than "Congrats!" You've seen their love story unfold; here's how to capture it in a message they'll save forever.

Tips for Writing Wedding Messages for Friend

What to Keep in Mind

  • Make It Personal: You know this person. Reference your friendship, a memory of them with their partner, or something specific about their relationship that you admire.
  • Match the Relationship: Close friends deserve heartfelt messages. Casual friends might appreciate something lighter. Write in a voice they'd recognize as you.
  • Look Forward: Wedding cards are about the future. Wish them specific joys: laughter, adventures, someone to binge Netflix with. Make it real.

Short Wedding Messages

When you want something brief but meaningful:

"So happy for you both! Here's to a lifetime of love and laughter."
"You two are perfect together. Congratulations on making it official!"
"Watching you find your person has been the best. So happy for you!"

Medium-Length Messages

For when you want to say a bit more:

"Congratulations! From the first time you told me about [partner's name], I knew this one was different. So happy to see you this happy. Here's to forever."
"You two just make sense. The way you look at each other, the way you make each other laugh—it's the real thing. Wishing you a lifetime of that magic."
"I've watched your love story unfold, and I couldn't be happier for you. You deserve every bit of this joy. Congratulations to you both!"

Heartfelt Messages

When you want to express deeper feelings:

"There aren't many people in this world who get to find their person. You found yours, and I've had a front-row seat to the kind of love that makes you believe in love. I'm so proud of you and so happy for you both."
"Remember when you weren't sure [partner's name] liked you back? And now look—you're getting married. Life is wild and beautiful, and I'm so glad you found each other. Love you both."
"You're one of my favorite people marrying one of your favorite people, which makes this one of my favorite days. Thank you for letting me be part of it. Here's to your forever."
"I've seen you through the bad dates, the heartbreaks, and the "I'm going to be single forever" rants. And now I get to see you marry the person who makes all of that worth it. I'm crying. Congratulations."

When to Send Your Wedding Card

Wedding cards should be sent or delivered around the wedding day. If attending the reception, bring your card with your gift or place it in the designated card box. If mailing, send it within a week of the wedding—to their home address, not the venue.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I include money or a check in the card?

Cash or checks are perfectly appropriate wedding gifts, especially if that's what the couple prefers. Tuck it securely in the card. Most couples appreciate contributions toward their new life together.

What if I only know one half of the couple?

Address the card to both, but it's okay if your message focuses more on your friend: "[Friend's name], I'm so happy for you both. [Partner's name] is lucky to have you—and clearly has great taste."

Can I write something funny?

Absolutely—if that matches your friendship. A well-placed joke can make a card memorable. Just balance humor with a genuine sentiment so it doesn't read as dismissive of the occasion.
