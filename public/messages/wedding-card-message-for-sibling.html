Wedding Card Messages for a Sibling

Updated February 2026 • 4 min read

Your brother or sister is getting married—the kid who used to steal your toys is now pledging their life to someone. It's weird and wonderful and maybe you're crying a little. Here's how to write a card that captures all those feelings.

Tips for Writing Wedding Messages for Sibling

What to Keep in Mind

  • Balance Sibling Humor with Heart: A little teasing is expected between siblings, but this is also a major life moment. Open with a joke if you want, but land on something real.
  • Welcome Their Partner: This is your chance to officially welcome your new in-law into the family. Acknowledge that they're not just marrying your sibling—they're joining your family.
  • Share a Sibling Perspective: You've known them their whole life. Share something only a sibling could: a childhood memory, how you've watched them grow, what makes them a great partner.

Short Wedding Messages

When you want something brief but meaningful:

"From annoying sibling to happily married—who saw that coming? Congratulations!"
"So happy for you both. Welcome to the family, [partner's name]!"
"My baby [brother/sister] is all grown up. I'm not crying. Congratulations!"

Medium-Length Messages

For when you want to say a bit more:

"I've been there through the braces, the bad haircuts, and the questionable fashion phases. Now I get to be here for this—the best day yet. So proud of you. Congratulations!"
"Watching you find your person has been one of the best things. [Partner's name], you're officially family now, and we couldn't be happier about it. Welcome!"
"Who would've thought my [brother/sister] would be the one to figure out this whole love thing? I'm so happy for you both. Here's to your forever."

Heartfelt Messages

When you want to express deeper feelings:

"Growing up, I never thought I'd say this, but... I'm really proud of the person you've become. The way you love [partner's name], the way they love you—it's the real thing. I'm so happy you found each other. Congratulations."
"I've watched you grow from the kid who [childhood memory] to the incredible person you are today. And now I get to watch you start this new chapter with someone who clearly adores you. Love you both so much."
"To my [brother/sister]: you deserve every bit of this happiness. And to [partner's name]: thank you for loving them the way they deserve. Welcome to our messy, wonderful family."
"They say you can't choose your family, but [partner's name] chose ours, and I couldn't be happier about it. Congratulations to you both. Here's to a lifetime of love, laughter, and putting up with our family's quirks."

When to Send Your Wedding Card

Bring your card to the wedding and place it in the card box, or give it to your sibling privately if you want a more personal moment. You might also consider writing a second card for the rehearsal dinner if you're giving a speech—something just for them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I share childhood stories in my card?

Brief, sweet stories are perfect. Save the embarrassing tales for your speech—the card should focus on love and congratulations, even if you add a touch of sibling humor.

How do I address the card?

Address it to both your sibling and their partner: "To [Sibling] and [Partner]" shows you're welcoming them as a couple.

What if I'm not close with my sibling's partner?

This is a good opportunity to build that relationship. Express genuine happiness for your sibling and a warm welcome to their partner. You don't have to be best friends to be family.
